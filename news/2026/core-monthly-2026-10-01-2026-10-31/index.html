    PrestaShop Core Monthly - October 2026

    A maturing Admin API, a wave of performance improvements, and new features on the develop branch

    Written by
    Marco Salvatore
    Solution Engineer
    Published

    Nov 3, 2026

    Share this

    Welcome to the October 2026 Core Monthly. With PrestaShop 9.2 out of the door, October was a month of consolidation: the Admin API took a big step towards maturity, the performance work picked up speed, and the 9.2.x branch collected a solid batch of fixes.

    Dear community,

    After a month as intense as September, you might expect things to slow down a little. They did not, they just changed direction. Instead of shipping a big release, October was about listening to the first feedback on 9.2, hardening what we just published, and preparing the ground for what comes next on develop. In total, 170 pull requests were merged by 31 contributors, and a good share of them came from the community. Here is what stood out.

    The Admin API is growing up

    The Admin API was one of the busiest areas of the month, and most of that work was about turning a promising feature into a contract that module developers and integrators can rely on. @jolelievre updated ADR 0023 to write down the rules the API now follows: resources live in the ps_apiresources module, lists share the same paginated format, and bulk operations and status codes behave consistently everywhere. Writing these decisions down matters, because it is what lets several people add endpoints in parallel without each of them reinventing the conventions.

    Concretely, every endpoint now requires a scope, which closes the door to resources accidentally exposed without permission checks. The missing Product endpoints were added, the experimental multistore support got its own documentation section, and a scheduled workflow now keeps the API tracking issue up to date every day, so anyone can see at a glance what is covered and what is still missing. On the community side, @PrestaEdit consolidated the Country, Customer and ImageType endpoints into single domain pull requests, which makes them much easier to review and merge.

    You will also notice a few PRs tagged “AI context” in the list below. The core and ps_apiresources repositories now ship context files, review checklists and conventions extracted from past reviews, so that AI assistants (and humans!) contributing to the codebase start from the same rules the maintainers apply. It is a natural follow-up to the work we described in Teaching AI to speak PrestaShop.

    Performance, one query at a time

    Benjamin Bendaoui (@LameuleFR) had a very productive month hunting down unnecessary work, and it did not go unnoticed: he also joined the list of project committers in October, which means he can now review and merge contributions too. Welcome aboard, Benjamin! On develop, the translator now only warms up the catalogues of installed languages, shop translations are saved with one query per language instead of one per string, upgrading a single module no longer loads every module present on disk, and the front office skips the cart quantity query when the cart has not even been saved yet. On the 9.2.x side, the out-of-stock label now reuses the quantities that are already computed, and in the Faceted search module uncached category pages run fewer and lighter SQL queries.

    None of these changes is spectacular on its own, and that is precisely the point: performance on a real shop is rarely about one big fix, it is about removing dozens of small costs that add up on every page view. If you have spotted a slow area in your own shop, this is a great example of how to contribute a targeted, easy-to-review improvement.

    New features on the develop branch

    While 9.2.x received fixes, develop kept moving towards the next version, and the B2B initiative took another step forward. Business entities are the companies behind B2B customers, stored in their own data model so that existing B2C shops are not affected. After the creation form earlier this year, @soledis-contributeur added the edit and deletion pages in the back office, so merchants can now manage business entities from start to finish.

    Carrier tracking URLs can now use order and delivery address placeholders, which is handy for carriers that need more than a tracking number to show the parcel status. @Codencode added automatic cleanup of outdated sessions, so the sessions table no longer grows forever, and the Docker Compose setup now accepts environment variables, making it possible to run several PrestaShop instances side by side when you develop or test.

    Modules, themes and tooling

    The ps_onepagecheckout module keeps getting polished after its debut in 9.2: the payment section and the footer are now aligned with the checkout form on desktop, and the configuration page warns merchants when guest checkout is disabled. The Product Comments module 9.0.1 and the Email subscription module 3.0.1 were released, the latter with missing database indexes contributed by @Prestaplugins. @kpodemski replaced the old webpack, Babel and node-sass toolchain of the faceted search with esbuild and Dart Sass, a welcome modernization for anyone building the module’s assets.

    PrestaShop Flashlight also had a busy month, with @Alexandre-Lematre fixing builds for older PrestaShop versions, the Alpine Apache images and the nginx routing of language-prefixed URLs.

    See you in Lyon

    PS Summit 2026 kicks off this week: the workshop and hackathon day on November 4th brings PrestaShop and Sylius developers together, and the conference opens on November 5th at the Palais des Congrès in Lyon. If you are attending, come and say hello to the team. We will share a recap of the event in the coming weeks.

    Most active contributors

    1. @Codencode (17 PRs) topped the list with a long series of fixes: multistore product deletion, module uninstall consistency, carrier price ranges showing the wrong currency, PHP 8.5 deprecations, the customization weight counted twice in orders, plus the automatic session cleanup on develop.
    2. @jolelievre (15 PRs) drove the Admin API work across the core, ps_apiresources and the ADR repository, set up the AI context files, and maintained the Docker images.
    3. @Quetzacoalt91 (14 PRs) kept the Update Assistant in sync with 9.2.0, released productcomments 9.0.1, and took care of Docker and CI updates.
    4. @boo-code (10 PRs) fixed the session handler lifetime, logo attachments in emails, a reserved stock underflow, TinyMCE sizing in hidden tabs and full name filtering in grids.
    5. @Alexandre-Lematre (10 PRs) worked almost entirely on PrestaShop Flashlight, making its images more reliable across PHP and PrestaShop versions.

    Spotlight on community contributors

    Every month we highlight a few contributors from outside the core team whose work made a difference. Here are five of them for October.

    • @Codencode was the most active contributor of the month, fixing multistore, module and carrier issues in the back office and order weight calculations in the front office, and clarifying the MaterialChoiceTableType documentation.
    • @boo-code once again touched every corner of the codebase, from sessions and emails to stock, webservice invoice numbers and the PrestaShop UI Kit icon font.
    • @LameuleFR delivered a series of performance improvements in the core and the faceted search, added address placeholders to carrier tracking URLs, and became a project committer.
    • @Hlavtox fixed UTF-8 feature values in structured data, zero as a valid multilingual value, the context country for automatic cart rules, and the new 9.2 product conditions in the faceted search.
    • @soledis-contributeur pushed the B2B initiative forward with the business entity edit and deletion pages in the back office.
    boo-code Codencode Hlavtox LameuleFR soledis-contributeur

    There is more in the list below than we could fit here: @ShaiMagal fixed a product page crash when the brand no longer exists, @lucasell95 fixed the customer groups cache, and @rootvector2 made the webservice date range filter include its boundaries. Take a scroll, there is something for everyone.
